## Keynote Overview
Testing the unprecedented without losing your mind or your capital, the question most founders cannot answer in sixty seconds.

## Core Keynote Thesis & Field Notes

In lectures to economics and startup-focused student audiences, I ask a simple question: what is the cheapest experiment that can conclusively disprove your foundational assumption? If a founder cannot answer that within sixty seconds, they are not validating, they are gambling with other people's capital.

To test the unprecedented without incinerating runway, I walk students through three disciplines: decoupling market demand from software maturity, establishing pre-mortem invalidation covenants that force a kill decision before emotional attachment clouds judgment, and optimizing for mean time to behavioral signal, because synthetic surveys lie and customer credit card authorizations do not.

This is the talk I enjoy giving most, because student audiences have not yet learned to perform confidence they do not have, which makes the validation discipline land harder.

## Key Takeaways for Founders, CEOs & Boards

- If you cannot name the cheapest experiment to disprove your core assumption in sixty seconds, you are gambling, not validating.
- Pre-mortem invalidation covenants should be set before building, not after emotional attachment forms.
- Real behavioral signal, like a payment, beats a synthetic survey every time.

## Executive Audience Q&A

### Q: Why speak to students instead of only industry audiences?

Because student audiences have not yet learned to perform confidence they do not have, which makes a lesson about honest validation land harder.

### Q: What's the one question you want every founder in the room to be able to answer?

What is the cheapest experiment that could conclusively disprove your core assumption, and can you name it in under sixty seconds.

## Keynote FAQs

### Q: What is a "pre-mortem invalidation covenant"?

A quantitative metric defined in advance that forces a founder to kill a feature or pivot a vertical before emotional attachment to the idea can cloud that decision.
