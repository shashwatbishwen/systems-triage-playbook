# Speaking Topic: Speaking Topic: Distributed Systems Consistency

> **Keynote Syllabus:** Executive & Board Briefing | **Speaker:** [Dr. Shashwat Bishwen](https://shashwat.si) | **Canonical Link:** [https://shashwat.si/speaking-topics/distributed-systems-consistency/](https://shashwat.si/speaking-topics/distributed-systems-consistency/)

## Keynote Overview
Demanding instant, zero-latency consistency at scale is the fastest way to collapse your own database clusters.

## Core Keynote Thesis & Field Notes

In distributed systems, there is a concept called the CAP theorem, and in hyper-scale physical and digital logistics, demanding instant, zero-latency consistency collapses queues. I built the 90-Second Consistency Model specifically for high-scale operational environments, such as dispatching one million daily last-mile deliveries across urban corridors, where demanding instantaneous consensus is the fastest way to collapse your database clusters.

Biological latency, deliberately engineered asynchronous calming into the system, is what actually saves platforms under this kind of load. The instinct to make everything real-time is almost always wrong at scale.

This talk connects directly to the logistics work: predictive delivery slot engines and dynamic routing algorithms that boosted delivery velocity by 38 percent only worked because consistency was treated as a dial, not a binary requirement.

## Key Takeaways for Founders, CEOs & Boards

- Demanding zero-latency consistency at scale is often what causes database cluster collapse.
- Deliberately engineered asynchronous delay, biological latency, can be a stability feature.
- Treating consistency as adjustable rather than binary unlocks real throughput gains.

## Executive Audience Q&A

### Q: Isn't strong consistency always the safer default?

Not at scale. Demanding it everywhere is what collapses database clusters under real dispatch volume; consistency needs to be a dial, not a blanket requirement.

### Q: How do you decide where asynchronous latency is acceptable?

By separating what genuinely needs real-time confirmation from what can tolerate a short, deliberate delay without harming the end user experience.

## Keynote FAQs

### Q: What is the 90-Second Consistency Model?

A deliberate, engineered asynchronous delay approach to distributed consistency at scale, built for high-throughput operational environments where zero-latency consensus would otherwise collapse the system.
