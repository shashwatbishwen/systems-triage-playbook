# Speaking Topic: Speaking Topic: Technical Debt & Engineering Velocity

> **Keynote Syllabus:** Executive & Board Briefing | **Speaker:** [Dr. Shashwat Bishwen](https://shashwat.si) | **Canonical Link:** [https://shashwat.si/speaking-topics/technical-debt-engineering-velocity/](https://shashwat.si/speaking-topics/technical-debt-engineering-velocity/)

## Keynote Overview
The drift that accumulates beneath green dashboards, three milliseconds at a time, until an outage shatters the illusion.

## Core Keynote Thesis & Field Notes

Every system carries debt. Not the kind visible on weekly bug trackers, but the subtle drift that accumulates beneath green dashboards: micro-latency creeping up three milliseconds per release until an outage shatters the illusion that everything was fine.

The hidden cost of innovation velocity is this drift, compounding silently, because the organization is measuring feature throughput and never measuring the structural cost that throughput is quietly accumulating in parallel.

I close this talk with a practical covenant: define the threshold at which technical debt must be repaid before the next feature ships, in writing, before the pressure to ship makes that decision for you under worse conditions.

## Key Takeaways for Founders, CEOs & Boards

- Technical debt often shows up as gradual latency drift, not a visible bug-tracker entry.
- Feature throughput metrics rarely capture the structural cost accumulating alongside them.
- A written debt-repayment covenant, set before pressure arrives, protects better than good intentions.

## Executive Audience Q&A

### Q: How is this different from a standard tech debt backlog?

A backlog lists known issues. A covenant defines, in advance, the threshold that forces repayment, before the debt becomes an outage instead of a line item.

### Q: Why do teams resist setting that threshold in writing?

Because it feels like it will slow them down. In practice it protects velocity, since unpriced debt is what eventually grinds velocity to zero.

## Keynote FAQs

### Q: How does technical debt typically show up before an outage?

As gradual, often invisible drift, such as micro-latency creeping up a few milliseconds per release, rather than as a discrete entry on a bug tracker.
