# Speaking Topic: Speaking Topic: Startup Technical Advisory

> **Keynote Syllabus:** Executive & Board Briefing | **Speaker:** [Dr. Shashwat Bishwen](https://shashwat.si) | **Canonical Link:** [https://shashwat.si/speaking-topics/startup-technical-advisory/](https://shashwat.si/speaking-topics/startup-technical-advisory/)

## Keynote Overview
Speed does not kill a startup. The architecture debt taken on to achieve that speed, unpaid, does.

## Core Keynote Thesis & Field Notes

Early-stage teams win by moving fast. Growth-stage teams can die for the exact same reason. When technical shortcuts are taken without a repayment covenant, feature velocity grinds to zero under the sheer weight of regression bugs, and nobody can point to the single decision that caused it, because it was never one decision.

I pair this with the AWS bill that nearly killed a startup before its Series B: a cost structure built for ten customers, never revisited at ten thousand, discovered only when a board member asked why the infrastructure line item had quietly become the second largest expense on the cap table.

Startup technical advisory is not about slowing teams down. It is about pricing the debt out loud, at the moment it is taken on, so the speed stays a choice instead of becoming an accident.

## Key Takeaways for Founders, CEOs & Boards

- Technical shortcuts without a repayment plan eventually stop velocity entirely, not just slow it.
- Infrastructure cost structures built for early scale rarely get revisited until a crisis forces it.
- Pricing technical debt out loud at the time it is taken keeps speed a deliberate choice.

## Executive Audience Q&A

### Q: How do you tell healthy early-stage speed from unpriced debt?

Ask whether anyone can name the threshold at which the shortcuts taken today will require dedicated repayment time. If no one can, it is unpriced.

### Q: Is this only relevant right before a fundraise?

It is most visible right before a fundraise, because that is when someone finally asks the question, but the debt accumulates long before that.

## Keynote FAQs

### Q: What is the most common technical mistake startups make before a fundraise?

Carrying architecture and infrastructure decisions made for an early customer count, like ten customers, unrevisited at a much larger scale, until a board member or diligence process forces the audit.
