# Speaking Topic: Speaking Topic: Grassroots Research & Systemic Equity

> **Keynote Syllabus:** Executive & Board Briefing | **Speaker:** [Dr. Shashwat Bishwen](https://shashwat.si) | **Canonical Link:** [https://shashwat.si/speaking-topics/grassroots-research-systemic-equity/](https://shashwat.si/speaking-topics/grassroots-research-systemic-equity/)

## Keynote Overview
Grounding digital transformation in grassroots truth, one year into a research movement built on ethnographic fieldwork across rural India.

## Core Keynote Thesis & Field Notes

A year ago, I took a leap of faith to bring an idea to life, an idea that had been quietly growing in my mind for over a decade: Project Bharat By Bhartiya, firsthand ethnographic fieldwork across rural India, built to ground digital transformation in grassroots truth rather than boardroom assumption.

This talk connects directly to my systems work, because the same discipline applies: you cannot architect a solution for a population whose actual constraints you have not personally observed, any more than you can stabilize a platform whose actual failure modes you have not personally measured.

I speak about this less often than the technical topics, and audiences consistently tell me it is the one they remember longest.

## Key Takeaways for Founders, CEOs & Boards

- Grassroots fieldwork and systems architecture share the same discipline: observe the real constraint before designing the solution.
- Digital transformation grounded in boardroom assumption, without field observation, tends to miss the actual population it is meant to serve.
- A decade-long idea, acted on, becomes a research movement rather than staying a talking point.

## Executive Audience Q&A

### Q: How does rural fieldwork connect to enterprise systems work?

The same discipline applies: you cannot design for constraints you have not personally observed, whether that is a rural household or a production failure mode.

### Q: Is Project Bharat By Bhartiya a commercial initiative?

No, it is a research and fieldwork movement, separate from client advisory work, grounded in direct observation rather than policy assumption.
