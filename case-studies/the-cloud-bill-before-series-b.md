# Case Study: Case Study: The Cloud Bill Before Series B

> **Client Profile:** A venture-backed, early-growth-stage startup preparing for a Series B raise. | **Author:** [Dr. Shashwat Bishwen](https://shashwat.si) | **Canonical Link:** [https://shashwat.si/case-studies/the-cloud-bill-before-series-b/](https://shashwat.si/case-studies/the-cloud-bill-before-series-b/)

## Problem Context
An infrastructure cost structure sized for ten customers, never revisited at ten thousand, surfaced during fundraise diligence.

## The Problem Architecture

A board member flagged that the infrastructure line item had quietly become the second largest expense on the cap table, with no architectural review to explain why. The company's cloud bill was on pace to approach 20 million dollars annualized, a number that would have materially damaged the Series B narrative if left unexplained.

## Technical Intervention & Approach

The architecture was audited against the customer count and usage pattern it was originally designed for, versus the scale it was now running at. The findings decoupled market demand validation from software maturity: parts of the system were over-engineered for a scale the product had not reached, while other parts were silently paying a premium for capacity nobody was using.

## Verified Measurable Outcomes & ROI

The cost structure was rebuilt around actual usage patterns rather than the assumptions made when the company had ten customers. The fundraise proceeded with a credible, audited infrastructure cost narrative instead of an unexplained budget line that diligence would otherwise have flagged.

### Key Performance Proof

- **Annualized cloud spend at risk:** `~$20M`
- **Engagement outcome:** `Audited cost narrative for Series B`

## Architectural Lessons & FAQs

### Q: Why do infrastructure costs become a fundraise risk?

When a cost structure built for an early customer count is never revisited at scale, the resulting unexplained spend becomes a diligence red flag precisely when a company can least afford one.
